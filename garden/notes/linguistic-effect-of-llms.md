# How LLMs are shifting human language

> Working notes: rough, evolving, and mostly for me. Expect half-finished thoughts.

These are reading notes from the systematic review I'm running.

## What counts as "LLM exposure"?

Studies operationalize this wildly differently:

- direct use (you prompt a model and copy its output)
- ambient exposure (you read text other people generated with a model)
- secondhand diffusion (a word spreads offline after starting online)

## The "delve" problem

The classic example is the spike in words like *delve*, *intricate*, and *underscore* in academic writing. But frequency alone doesn't prove causation; you need a baseline, and most papers don't have one. The cleanest evidence so far is [Kobak et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016), who measured "excess vocabulary" across 14 million PubMed abstracts and found an abrupt post-2022 jump in exactly these style words, estimating that at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts were LLM-processed.

## Open questions

- How to measure change in **spoken** language, not just written?
- Where is the line between a tool helping you write and a tool writing *for* you?
