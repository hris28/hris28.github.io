What your browser silently sees

20 Jun 2026 · privacy, security, web

A rabbit hole I keep falling back into: how much can a website learn about me just by my showing up? No login, no form--just opening the page. The answer is "a startling amount," and the unsettling part is that these signals combine into a near-unique fingerprint that follows you even with cookies cleared.[1] So I built something to read it all back to you--running entirely in your browser.

What this is actually showing

None of this required permission. The browser hands it over to make pages work--your screen size so layouts fit, your language so text localizes, your GPU so graphics render. Individually it's harmless. Combined, the set of values is distinctive enough that researchers found most browsers are uniquely identifiable from these traits alone--a technique called browser fingerprinting.[2] Unlike a cookie, you can't delete a fingerprint.

How real websites go further

This demo stops at what your browser volunteers. Commercial trackers add layers on top:

  • IP geolocation - your address maps to a city/region and your internet provider.
  • Cross-site tracking - the same third-party scripts on many sites stitch your visits together.[1]
  • Tracking pixels - invisible 1×1 images that report when you opened an email or page.
  • Third-party cookies - persistent IDs shared across sites (now being phased down, which is why fingerprinting is rising).

How consent levels work

That cookie banner isn't decoration. Choices usually map to tiers, each unlocking more collection: Essential (basic data needed to function) → + Functional (preferences, storage) → + Analytics (behavior, performance) → + Marketing (full cross-site profiling). "Reject all" really does collect less.

The URL itself is tracking you

Checking this page's address for tracking parameters…

Those utm_source, utm_campaign, and gclid tags appended to links tell a site exactly which ad, email, or post sent you. Try adding ?utm_source=garden&utm_campaign=demo to this page's URL and reload — the line above will read it back.

What you can actually do

  • Use a tracker-blocking browser or extension (uBlock Origin; Firefox or Brave with strong defaults).
  • Turn on Global Privacy Control / "Do Not Track" where available.
  • Block third-party cookies in your browser settings.
  • A VPN hides your IP and approximate location (it doesn't stop fingerprinting).
  • Test yourself against a real tracker with EFF's Cover Your Tracks.[1]
